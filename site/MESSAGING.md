# HFZ messaging framework

Built bottom-up from the evidence, per the four-tier hierarchy (Kingman). Tier 4
first, because a value proposition written from aspiration usually turns out to be
unsupported.

## Why the previous page was unclear

Diagnosed against the framework's list of structural failures:

1. **No value proposition at all.** The page listed what HFZ sells (domain,
   hosting, email, websites, SEO). That is a *category description* — every agency
   in Malaysia could claim it. It fails the exclusivity test.
2. **Tier conflation.** "Every price on the page" was an assertion dressed as a
   proof point. The proof is *48 published prices including renewal prices*; the
   page never said that number.
3. **Orphaned evidence.** The 50/40/10 schedule, ownership in the client's name,
   and the stated support hours were all on the page but connected to no claim.
   True, compelling, and doing no work.
4. **Labels instead of claims.** "The rate card", "The rest of the list" are
   headings, not arguments. A reader learns the shape of the page, not why to care.
5. **The fear is never named.** See below.

## Tier 4 — Reasons to believe (the existing evidence base)

Everything here already exists and is verifiable on the page or in the terms.

- 48 published prices, including the renewal column most agencies hide
- Payment schedule fixed at 50 / 40 / 10 and published
- Domain, hosting and files registered in the client's name
- Written scope and design approval before work starts
- Maintenance plans with stated support hours (2h Business, 5h Professional)
- A physical address in Cheras and a phone number, not just a form
- Prices well below the international category anchor for a comparable scope

## Voice of customer (what owners actually say)

From r/smallbusiness threads on hiring a web agency:

| What they say | What it means for the page |
|---|---|
| "It was 5k... nominally better than my Squarespace site." | Price and quality are assumed to be unrelated. Show what the money buys. |
| "I wanted to add two pages and they wanted $1800." | The change-order fear. Name the per-page price up front. |
| "They use $10-20/month builders then upcharge $1000-4000." | The reseller accusation. Publish the hosting cost separately from the build. |
| "If an agency won't tell you what you're paying for, that's your answer." | Opacity reads as guilt. This is the strongest argument for publishing. |
| "Never hire from a DM." | Cold outreach reads as scam. Being findable and local is the counter. |
| "95% of web designers are dogshit at SEO." | Do not bundle SEO and imply it is included. Price it separately. |
| "A prefab site with great content outperforms a beautiful site with average content." | Lead with enquiries, not aesthetics. |

The fear underneath all of it: **you will be quoted one number, invoiced another,
and left holding a website you do not control.**

## Tier 2 — Strategic claims

Four. Each tested for plausibility (would a sceptical owner believe it?) and
distinctiveness (could a competitor claim it?).

| # | Claim | Plausible | Distinctive |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The price is published before you call | Yes, it is on the page | Yes. Almost no Malaysian agency publishes a rate card |
| 2 | The quote does not move | Yes, if the scope is written | Partly. Distinctive only because it is paired with a published rate |
| 3 | You own the domain, the hosting and the files | Yes | No. A category entry condition. Assert it, do not lead with it |
| 4 | The site is built to be found, and SEO is priced separately | Yes | Yes. Most competitors bundle "SEO" and imply it is included |

Claim 3 stays in the architecture but is deliberately not the headline, because it
fails distinctiveness. Claims 1 and 2 are the differentiators.

## Tier 1 — Core value proposition

Tested against both structural rules.

**Abstraction test:** must cover all four claims without contradicting any. It does.

**Exclusivity test (the substitution test):** read it to a competitor and ask if
they would claim it. They would not, because claiming it requires publishing a
price list and holding a fixed quote.

> HFZ removes the risk from buying a website. The price is published before you
> call, the quote is fixed once the scope is written, and the domain, hosting and
> files end up in your name.

## Derived assets

**Headline:**

> If you can imagine it,
> *we can build it.*

This replaces "The prices are on this page. They do not change after you call."

**Why the change.** That line was the CVP compressed, and it was the wrong job
for the h1. It is a trust statement, and trust statements read as defensive —
leading with "they do not change" implies someone expected them to. It also
spent the most valuable line on the page restating something the reader has not
yet been given a reason to care about.

The page already has a better home for that argument: **the claims band sits
immediately below the hero** and states all four claims with their proof. So the
division of labour is now:

- **h1 — the aspiration.** Why a website, and why with us.
- **claims band — the proof.** Published prices, fixed quote, ownership, SEO
  priced separately.

Aspiration, then evidence. Leading with evidence was the mistake.

The two earlier versions of the headline are both in this file's history: the
original "See the price before you call. Then a quote that does not move." was
cut for being a metaphor doing rhetorical work; the replacement was cut for
being a trust statement in a slot that needs a reason to read on. Do not put a
price claim back in the h1 — the hero rail already carries RM1,500 / RM500 /
RM59, and the claims band carries the argument.

**Eyebrow:** Website prices for Malaysian SMEs · Cheras, Kuala Lumpur

**The claims band** (the four claims with their proof attached, directly under the
hero, so the argument is stated before the evidence):

| Claim | Proof |
|---|---|
| Every price is on this page | 48 of them, renewals included |
| The quote does not change | Scope in writing · 50 / 40 / 10 |
| The domain and hosting are yours | Registered in your name |
| SEO is priced separately | From RM800 a month |

Two of these were rewritten. "You own everything" failed the concreteness test —
*everything* is not a claim, it is a mood. "SEO is priced, not implied" was a
binary contrast, which is the single most common AI tell; it became the flat
statement of fact.

## Copy rules

The register is a shopkeeper explaining prices, not a brand asserting values.
Every rule below is enforced on the page.

1. **No binary contrast.** "Not X, but Y" / "X, not Y" / "It isn't just X, it's
   Y". State what the thing is. This was the most frequent tell on the page.
2. **No negative listing.** "None of them is an upsell, and none of them is a
   redesign." Cut it and say the positive version, or cut it entirely.
3. **No faux-insight setups.** "The renewal price is the one people get
   surprised by" frames the writer as the lone truth-teller. State the fact.
4. **No metaphor standing in for a plain verb.** "Does not move" → "does not
   change". "Priced up front" → "one-off technical work".
5. **No abstract-noun headings.** "The recurring costs, in advance" became
   "Maintenance, add-ons and SEO" — the actual contents.
6. **No em-dashes in body copy.** Colons for lists and labels, commas elsewhere.
7. **No tricolons for rhythm.** Meta lines state two facts, not three.
8. **Never repeat a sentence's content in its meta line.** The step paragraph
   and the meta under it must carry different information.
9. **Show, don't tell.** Section 02 shows four before/after miniatures instead
   of describing four failures. Section 06 shows an actual scope sheet instead
   of describing a fixed quote. If a claim can be a table, a number or a
   document, it must not be a sentence.

Rule 9 is the load-bearing one. The rest are hygiene.

**What was removed:** the intro paragraph on the hero (it described the
category), the "what this costs" margin note (orphaned claim), the second person
plural "we build and maintain websites for Malaysian SMEs" line, "Nothing is
assumed into the price" (negative flourish), and the "You own everything" step
meta (repeated the paragraph above it).

## Deployment rule going forward

Every claim on the page must be adjacent to its proof. If a sentence cannot be
laddered up to one of the four claims, or down to a number, it is noise and gets
cut. If it can be shown instead of said, it gets shown.
